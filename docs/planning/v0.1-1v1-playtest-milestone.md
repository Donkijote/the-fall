# V0.1 1v1 Playtest Milestone

Status: Confirmed

## Version meaning

`V0.1` names the first friends-and-family playtest phase after the completed V0 foundation and first
playable. It is a project milestone and candidate identifier, not a public semantic-release promise,
store version, production-support commitment, or content-complete demo.

## Player-visible outcome

A friend unfamiliar with the project can install one documented build, launch it, understand how to
start and play, complete one offline 1v1 bot match, replay or leave, and provide useful feedback
without Manuel coaching the primary session.

The milestone improves the proven game rather than adding Story Mode, another participant mode, or
online systems. Its purpose is to learn whether the current 1v1 experience is understandable,
readable, comfortable, and enjoyable outside the development environment.

## Why this slice

The first playable proves that the complete deterministic rules, bot orchestration, integrated table,
animation, audio, and application flow work. The physical-iPhone checkpoint also exposed a concrete
experience problem: desktop-derived content becomes too small on the phone even when it technically
fits inside the safe area.

The next uncertainty is therefore player usability, not rules completeness. A bounded 1v1 playtest
build can test visual hierarchy, phone readability, onboarding, interaction feedback, pacing, and
distribution before the project invests in Story Mode structure, additional modes, production assets,
or networking.

## Scope boundary

### Retained game

- offline 1v1 against the existing deterministic baseline bot
- the complete confirmed 1v1 rules and two existing setup options
- the authoritative domain, shared intents, fixed gameplay camera, and private-information boundary
- Home, setup, loading, match, result, replay, and return-to-Home
- existing functional animation, VFX, and audio as the starting presentation

No rule or bot-strategy expansion is required unless a playtest-blocking defect is found.

### Adaptive UI foundation

Desktop, mobile portrait, and mobile landscape are authored compositions that share game state and
design language. Mobile is not a uniformly scaled-down desktop layout.

The foundation must:

- audit Home, setup, match, and result at every accepted desktop resolution and on the recorded
  physical iPhone in portrait and landscape
- record measurable minimums for readable text, card identity, character identity, controls, and
  touch targets from actual device evidence
- define reusable typography, spacing, panel, button, focus, selection, semantic-state, and safe-area
  rules
- allocate phone space according to decision priority: local hand and actionable controls first,
  table state and score second, contextual explanation third, decorative content last
- preserve non-color-only feedback, keyboard focus, mouse interaction, touch interaction, privacy,
  orientation state, and the fixed camera

Passing safe-area containment is insufficient when content is technically visible but too small to
read or touch comfortably.

### Flow and match HUD

Home and setup provide an obvious primary action and explain the two configurable rules. Loading
communicates progress. Result identifies the winner and makes replay and return-to-Home unmistakable.

The match HUD prioritizes:

- local hand and available action
- active player, dealer, score, round/deal, and turn state
- canto opportunity, announcement, resolution, and penalty
- legal, selected, confirmed, rejected, and temporarily blocked interaction states
- Fall, clean table, capture, cascade, score, tie extension, and victory feedback

Names, scores, cards, controls, and semantic feedback must remain readable at the documented desktop
layouts and on the physical phone in both orientations.

### Onboarding and help

Provide lightweight, skippable help plus an always-available rules reference. A new player must be
able to discover the objective, controls, mandatory captures, cascades, Falls, clean tables, cantos,
scoring, victory, and setup choices without live coaching.

Help remains localization-ready, adapts prompts to the active input path, reveals no hidden
information, and never resolves or duplicates an intent. A Story Mode tutorial, strategic coaching,
voice-over, and a complete rule encyclopedia are outside V0.1.

### Presentation and essential settings

Refine existing timing, transitions, loading feedback, semantic VFX, and functional audio only where
they improve comprehension or comfort. Expose coherent volume and motion/playback controls and retain
appropriate preferences locally.

Normal, fast-forward, reduced-motion, skip, interruption, cancellation, replay, and teardown must
continue to converge on authoritative state. V0.1 does not require final music, voice, production VFX,
haptics, character acting, final characters, or production environment art.

### Distribution and feedback

Select one practical initial delivery lane based on the first tester's hardware before packaging.
Record the candidate commit, Unity version, platform, support boundary, installation, launch,
controls, known limitations, and recovery steps.

The exact distributed artifact must complete the full 1v1 flow. A structured feedback form records:

- whether the player understood the objective and next action
- desktop or mobile readability and touch/control comfort
- setup, match, canto, scoring, result, replay, and exit comprehension
- presentation pacing and enjoyment
- defects, build identifier, platform, and orientation or resolution

At least one external tester completes the primary session without live coaching. Feedback is grouped
as defects, usability findings, content requests, or future-scope ideas; accepted findings receive
focused issues rather than expanding the playtest issue indefinitely.

## Platform and performance boundary

The existing macOS universal development-player lane and Manuel's recorded physical-iPhone lane remain
the available validation evidence. The first external delivery lane is deliberately open until the
first tester's hardware is known; selecting it does not establish a production platform floor.

Issue [#42](https://github.com/Donkijote/the-fall/issues/42) remains a focused iPhone frame-pacing
follow-up. Its narrow p95 miss does not block qualitative V0.1 playtesting because the recorded run had
no frame over `100 ms`, memory or thermal failure, or authoritative mismatch. It must remain a known
limitation when relevant and be completed before claiming the recorded iPhone performance gate.

## Objective acceptance criteria

V0.1 is accepted when:

1. Home, setup, loading, match, and result use one coherent adaptive UI foundation.
2. Required desktop layouts and the recorded iPhone in portrait and landscape satisfy documented
   readability, hierarchy, safe-area, and control-size expectations.
3. A new player can identify the objective, next action, interaction result, score, and winner without
   developer explanation.
4. Lightweight onboarding and rules help are skippable, revisitable, input-aware, and consistent with
   the authoritative rules.
5. Volume and motion/playback controls are reachable, readable, and applied consistently.
6. Automated rule, integration, orientation, interaction, presentation, localization, and
   authoritative-state regressions pass.
7. One reproducible candidate can be installed and launched from written instructions without
   repository secrets or private developer data.
8. At least one external tester completes, replays or leaves a full match without coaching during the
   primary run.
9. Feedback and defects are recorded against the exact candidate and prioritized into focused
   follow-ups.
10. The project explicitly decides whether another V0.1 iteration is needed before choosing the next
    product milestone.

## Non-goals

- Story Mode structure, narrative, route hub, tournament, progression, rewards, or unlocks
- three-player, 2v2, local pass-and-play, or online PvP
- additional bot difficulty, strategic AI, accounts, profiles, cloud state, analytics, or monetization
- production-complete art, final characters, soundtrack, voice, VFX, haptics, or mastering
- public beta, store submission, marketing, broad device support, or production certification
- resolving every future accessibility, localization, platform, or distribution decision

## Exit decision

Completing V0.1 does not automatically start Story Mode. The playtest evidence determines whether the
1v1 experience needs another focused iteration or is stable enough to support a separately defined
product milestone. Story Mode, additional match modes, and online PvP remain visible future choices,
not implied follow-ons.
